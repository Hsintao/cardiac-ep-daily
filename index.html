Guidelines for the management of atrial fibrillation in adult patients admitted to an intensive care unit.

Ann Intensive Care·2026-10-19·PMID 42894798
房颤与消融 Journal Article

Vincent Labbé、Nadia Aissaoui、Pierre Asfar 等 14 位作者

英文摘要

Most existing recommendations for atrial fibrillation are derived from cardiology settings. However, atrial fibrillation is also highly prevalent among patients admitted to intensive care units and is associated with adverse clinical outcomes. Therefore, its management in this specific context warrants dedicated guidance. To address this need, The French Intensive Care Society has developed formalized expert recommendations using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) methodology. Twenty-four questions addressing the management of atrial fibrillation were evaluated in adult patients admitted to intensive care units. Of these, twenty-one focused specifically on new-onset atrial fibrillation, one addressed pre-existing atrial fibrillation, and two were applicable to both conditions. The assessment encompassed the role of transthoracic and transesophageal echocardiography, the management of potentially reversible triggers (optimization of fluid status, magnesium supplementation, serum potassium targets, and screening for thyroid dysfunction…), strategies for mitigating hemodynamic risk (indications and modalities for rhythm and rate control), and the management of thrombotic risk (indications, regimens, and duration of therapeutic anticoagulation). Eighteen recommendations were formulated, all of which achieved strong agreement among the expert panel. Only two recommendations were supported by a low level of evidence (GRADE 2+), whereas the remaining sixteen relied on evidence that could not be classified within the GRADE framework and were therefore issued as expert opinions. For six additional aspects of atrial fibrillation management, the experts concluded that the available evidence was insufficient to support the formulation of a reliable recommendation.

Determinants of outcome in atrial functional tricuspid regurgitation.

Echo Res Pract·2026-10-19·PMID 42894198
房颤与消融 Journal Article

Jawza Aldakhil、Kush Patel、Sveeta Badiani 等 6 位作者

英文摘要

BACKGROUND: Atrial functional tricuspid regurgitation (AFTR) is increasingly recognized as a result of atrial remodelling in patients with atrial fibrillation (AF). However, prognostic predictors in this population remain incompletely defined. OBJECTIVES: To evaluate echocardiographic characteristics and identify predictors of all-cause mortality in patients with AFTR. METHODS: We retrospectively studied consecutive patients with AF who underwent transthoracic echocardiography between 2017 and 2025. Inclusion required significant right atrial remodelling, defined as a right atrial area > 25 cm2. Patients with at least moderate functional tricuspid regurgitation comprised the AFTR cohort, while patients with mild/trivial/no tricuspid regurgitation served as controls. Patients with primary valvular disease, cardiomyopathy, congenital heart disease, device-related valve disease, RV dilatation, or more than mild RV systolic dysfunction were excluded. Cox proportional hazards regression was performed to identify predictors of mortality. RESULTS: A total of 152 patients were included, comprising 120 patients with AFTR and 32 controls. Compared with controls, patients with AFTR demonstrated larger atrial dimensions, higher right-sided hemodynamic-burden, impaired RV longitudinal strain, and reduced reservoir RA strain. During a median follow-up of 6.3 years, 53 deaths (45%) occurred among patients with AFTR. In univariable Cox analysis, age (HR 1.02, 95% CI 1.00-1.04; p = 0.016), TR Vmax (HR 1.59, 95% CI 1.07-2.35; p = 0.020), and lower reservoir RA strain (HR 0.90, 95% CI 0.84-0.96; p = 0.003) were associated with mortality. In multivariable analysis, reservoir RA strain remained independently associated with mortality (adjusted HR 0.88, 95% CI 0.81-0.95; p < 0.001). CONCLUSION: AFTR is characterized by marked biatrial remodelling and impaired right atrial function. Reduced RA reservoir strain was independently associated with mortality, supporting a potential role for RA deformation imaging in risk stratification in AFTR.

Distinct hemodynamic implications of junctional rhythm versus sinus rhythm on cardiac reverse remodeling after the Maze procedure.

J Cardiothorac Surg·2026-10-19·PMID 42894221
房颤与消融 Journal Article

Hee Jeong Lee、YoHan Bae、Jung Uk Woo 等 8 位作者

英文摘要

BACKGROUND: Surgical success after the Maze procedure is conventionally defined as "freedom from atrial fibrillation (AF)," often grouping junctional rhythm (JR) with normal sinus rhythm (NSR). However, the specific hemodynamic impact of JR remains poorly understood. This study investigated whether JR truly represents a successful outcome compared to NSR and AF. METHODS: We retrospectively analyzed patients undergoing the Maze procedure (2012-2024). After designating the first postoperative month as a blanking period for rhythm instability, we included only patients maintaining a consistent rhythm without crossover from 1 to 12 months postoperatively: AF (n = 71), NSR (n = 213), and JR (n = 35). Outcomes included cardiac reverse remodeling-assessed by left atrial contractile strain (LASct) and chamber dimensions-and permanent pacemaker (PPM) implantation. RESULTS: In the IPTW-adjusted cohort, the NSR group achieved significant cardiac reverse remodeling, characterized by improved LVEF (58.5%), reduced LA volume index (75.1 mL/m²), and notable recovery of LASct. In stark contrast, the JR group failed to show any reverse remodeling or mechanical recovery, exhibiting hemodynamic stagnation indistinguishable from the AF group. Consequently, no significant difference in cardiac function was observed between the JR and AF groups. Furthermore, the JR group faced a drastically elevated risk of PPM implantation compared to both the NSR and AF groups (P < 0.001). CONCLUSIONS: JR is not a benign alternative to sinus rhythm; it mimics AF in preventing cardiac reverse remodeling and carries a high risk of pacemaker dependency. Therefore, JR should be clinically differentiated from NSR and recognized as a suboptimal hemodynamic outcome similar to AF.

英文摘要

Iliac branch endovascular device thrombosis following blunt force trauma has rarely been reported. We describe an 81-year-old man who developed proximal aortobiiliac endograft collapse with subsequent thrombosis after blunt force trauma occurring two months following endovascular aneurysm repair. The patient delayed vascular follow-up for one year and later presented at routine surveillance with progressive bilateral hip and buttock claudication. Despite chronic therapeutic anticoagulation for atrial fibrillation, the patient developed progressive thrombosis with ischemic symptoms. The case highlights a rare mechanical cause of post-EVAR aortoiliac thrombosis and demonstrates the importance of early vascular imaging and consultation following traumatic injury in post-EVAR patients.

英文摘要

BACKGROUND: We previously demonstrated that AI-derived cardiac chamber volumetry in ECG-gated CT scans predicts atrial fibrillation (AF) and heart failure (HF). Since most chest CTs are non-gated, and the American Heart Association recently highlighted their value for opportunistic cardiovascular risk assessment using AI-based coronary artery calcium scoring, we extended this concept to evaluate whether AI-derived chamber volumetry from non-gated CTs can reliably predict HF and AF.s. METHODS: We compared AI-derived chamber volumetry from non-gated and ECG-gated chest CTs in 2053 Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA) Exam 5 participants using mean and standard-deviation of absolute differences, intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC), and Cohen's κ. CONCLUSION: AI-based evaluation of non-gated chest CT may provide reliable cardiac chamber volumetry and cardiovascular outcome prediction, enabling opportunistic screening for HF and AF risk.

GLP-1 receptor agonist therapy and incident atrial fibrillation in non-diabetic adults with obesity and chronic kidney disease: a propensity matched cohort study.

Abdulraheem Hassan、Efeturi Okorigba、Chilaka Grace 等 8 位作者

英文摘要

BACKGROUND: Obesity and chronic kidney disease (CKD) are potent risk factors for atrial fibrillation (AF), yet whether GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) prevent incident AF in non-diabetic adults with obesity and CKD is unexamined. We assessed whether GLP-1RA therapy reduces 1-year AF incidence in this cardiorenal phenotype. METHODS: Using the TriNetX US Collaborative Network (66 healthcare organizations), we conducted a retrospective target trial emulation of non-diabetic adults aged 18 or older with obesity and CKD stages 3-5 diagnosed 2021-2024. Patients receiving semaglutide, liraglutide, or tirzepatide (n = 10,985) were compared with unexposed patients (n = 94,527). After propensity score matching, 7326 patients remained per cohort. The primary outcome was incident AF at 1 year; secondary outcomes included AF subtypes, ventricular tachyarrhythmias, and all-cause hospitalization. Cox models generated hazard ratios (HRs); sensitivity analyses extended follow-up to 2-3 years with landmark analyses. RESULTS: At 1 year, GLP-1RA use was associated with significantly lower incident AF (1.63% vs. 2.62%; HR 0.655, 95% CI 0.508-0.844; p = 0.001), a 34.5% relative risk reduction. Reductions were consistent for paroxysmal (HR 0.586), persistent (HR 0.482), and longstanding persistent AF (HR 0.527; all p < 0.05); permanent AF was non-significant at 1 year. Ventricular tachyarrhythmias (HR 0.556; p = 0.002) and hospitalization (HR 0.668; p < 0.001) were also significantly reduced. Benefits persisted across 2- and 3-year follow-up and remained consistent in landmark analyses. CONCLUSIONS: In non-diabetic adults with obesity and CKD, GLP-1RA therapy was associated with significant, durable reductions in incident AF, ventricular arrhythmias, and hospitalization. These findings are consistent with a possible cardiorenal benefit and support prioritizing this phenotype for dedicated randomized trials.

Palpitations in children and adolescents in the emergency department: a state-of-the-art narrative review of arrhythmia risk, diagnostic strategy, and disposition.

Cardiol Young·2026-10-19·PMID 42894485
室性心律失常 Journal Article Review

Khaled Abouelmagd、Temitomi Jane Oyedele、Gabriela Berger 等 9 位作者

英文摘要

BACKGROUND: Palpitations are not an uncommon complaint in the paediatric emergency department, causing significant anxiety for families concerned about serious heart disease. However, most cases are benign, often stemming from normal physiological responses like fever or anxiety. Primary cardiac arrhythmias are rare in this clinical setting. PROBLEM: The main challenge is identifying the few children with dangerous arrhythmias among the many benign cases. Fear of missing a life-threatening condition often leads to extensive, costly, and stressful investigations, even in low-risk patients, without significantly improving detection rates. OBJECTIVE: This review aims to create a structured, risk-based framework for evaluating paediatric palpitations in the emergency department. The goal is to help clinicians efficiently distinguish benign from potentially life-threatening causes, thereby optimising resource use and reducing family anxiety. FINDINGS: A thorough clinical history, physical exam, and a standard 12-lead electrocardiogram are the most effective evaluation tools. "Red-flag" features-such as palpitations with fainting (syncope), onset during exercise, or a family history of sudden cardiac death-successfully identify nearly all children with significant arrhythmias. Barring certain risk factors and any abnormalities on a 12 lead electrocardiogram, a dangerous cardiac condition is highly unlikely. RECOMMENDATIONS: A stratified approach is recommended. Low-risk children (no red-flag features, normal electrocardiogram) can be safely discharged with reassurance. High-risk patients require immediate, aggressive evaluation, including cardiology consultation and potential hospital admission. This strategy improves accuracy, is cost-effective, and enhances patient safety.

Integration of an echocardiographic motion-management system into photon-based stereotactic arrhythmia radioablation planning.

Hans Oppitz、Amelie L Matusche、Laura Mantovani 等 18 位作者

英文摘要

BACKGROUND AND PURPOSE: Cardiorespiratory ultrasound motion monitoring may enhance the precision of dose delivery for stereotactic arrhythmia radioablation (STAR) of ventricular tachycardia. This treatment planning study aimed to evaluate the feasibility of photon-based STAR planning in the presence of an ultrasound probe. MATERIALS AND METHODS: For sixteen targets in fourteen patients, three treatment plans were retrospectively created and compared: a reference plan without an ultrasound probe and two plans in which the ultrasound probe was spared from the primary treatment beam (apical or parasternal probe positions). Descriptive and statistical analyses were performed for target/organ dose-volume and treatment beam metrics, conformity-indices and maximum probe exposure. RESULTS: The ultrasound probe could be spared from the primary beam in all cases with D0.035 cm³ < 1 Gy (median 0.2 Gy with apical and 0.4 Gy with parasternal placement) with slightly elevated organ dose-volume metrics and no significant differences in target dose-volume metrics. The conformity-index (50 % isodose line) differed significantly for apical and parasternal probe position (median reference plan 4.0 vs. 5.0 and 4.5, respectively; p < 0.01). The parasternal position gave slightly superior results compared to the apical position (e.g., heart minus planning target volume V12.5 Gy 7.6 % [4.7-13.3] vs. 8.2 % [6.4-12.5]; left lung V10 Gy 3.4 % [1.0-4.5] vs. 4.2 % [1.6-6.8]), albeit in all plans dose guidance parameters remained within clinically acceptable limits. CONCLUSIONS: The ultrasound probe could be spared from the primary beam without compromising photon-based STAR plan quality beyond clinically acceptable deviations. The results support further investigation of ultrasound-based STAR motion management.

英文摘要

Cardiac allograft vasculopathy (CAV) is a major cause of mortality and morbidity within the first 3 years of heart transplantation (HT). CAV results in progressive coronary artery stenosis and may lead to arrhythmias and sudden cardiac death. In some cases, sudden cardiac death represents the initial presentation of CAV, as cardiac denervation blunts anginal symptoms that otherwise could have preceded sudden cardiac death (SCD). Here, we describe the case of a 56-year-old man with non-ischemic cardiomyopathy. He underwent left ventricular assist device implantation followed by orthotopic HT. Am J Prev Cardiol·2026-10-18·PMID 42893154
影像与标测 Journal Article

Jacky Chen、Thomas H Marwick、Cheng Hwee Soh

英文摘要

AIM: Asymptomatic left ventricular dysfunction (LVD) represents a key and frequently under-recognized stage in the heart failure (HF) continuum. Early identification enables targeted prevention and timely initiation of evidence-based therapies. Artificial intelligence-enabled electrocardiography (AI-ECG) may provide a scalable alternative to imaging for LVD detection. This systematic review synthesized evidence from exclusively externally validated AI-ECG studies evaluating the detection of asymptomatic LVD, with symptomatic HF cohorts included to contextualize the evidence base across the HF continuum. METHODS: A systematic literature search was conducted on 8th September 2025. The main output was the pooled area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC). RESULTS: From 3,587 records, 12 asymptomatic and 30 symptomatic studies were identified (538,735 participants). In asymptomatic cohorts evaluating left ventricular systolic dysfunction (LVSD), AI-ECG demonstrated promising but heterogeneous discriminatory performance (I²>85%) with pooled AUROCs of 0.87 (95% CI 0.75-0.99) for LVEF ≤35%, 0.93 (95% CI 0.88-0.97) for LVEF ≤40%, and 0.89 (95% CI 0.85-0.97) for LVEF ≤50%. Asymptomatic left ventricular diastolic dysfunction (LVDD) demonstrated moderate performance (AUROC 0.76, 95% CI 0.66-0.86) with no externally validated symptomatic LVDD studies identified. Small-study effects were detected exclusively in asymptomatic cohorts. CONCLUSIONS: Discriminatory performance of AI-ECG in asymptomatic LVSD is broadly comparable to symptomatic populations. High risk of bias driven by prolonged ECG-to-echocardiogram intervals reflecting real-world logistical constraints necessitate cautious interpretation of results and further prospective validation.

Unmasking Brugada Syndrome With Oral Propafenone in a Resource-Limited Setting: A Case Report.

Cureus·2026-10-19·PMID 42894783
遗传与基础 Case Reports Journal Article

Luisa Fernanda Jaramillo Astudillo、Guillermo Adrian Navas Moreira、Patricia Delgado Cedeño 等 5 位作者

英文摘要

Brugada syndrome (BrS) is a cardiac channelopathy predisposing patients to malignant ventricular arrhythmias and sudden cardiac death (SCD) in the absence of structural heart disease. The diagnosis requires documentation of a type 1 Brugada electrocardiographic pattern, which can be unmasked using sodium channel-blocking agents in symptomatic patients presenting with non-diagnostic baseline patterns. We report a case of a 24-year-old male patient with a history of unexplained syncope who exhibited a non-diagnostic type 2 Brugada pattern on a baseline electrocardiogram (ECG). Due to the local unavailability of standard intravenous antiarrhythmics, a pharmacological provocation test was conducted using a single 600 mg oral dose of propafenone. Under continuous cardiac monitoring, the patient safely developed a diagnostic type 1 "coved" electrocardiographic pattern starting at the third hour, confirming the diagnosis of BrS. The ECG returned to its baseline pattern eight hours after the initiation of the test, and no cardiovascular complications occurred. This case highlights the clinical utility and safety of oral propafenone as an effective alternative diagnostic challenge for unmasking BrS in resource-limited environments. Furthermore, it emphasizes the necessity of utilizing extended monitoring protocols due to the specific pharmacokinetic profile and delayed onset of action of oral agents.

Channelrhodopsin ion selectivity determines mechanisms and efficacy of optogenetic defibrillation in human atria and ventricles.

J Physiol·2026-10-18·PMID 42892728
遗传与基础 Journal Article

Sophia Ohnemus、Albert Dasí、Ahmed F Ramadan 等 13 位作者

英文摘要

Cardiac re-entrant arrhythmias are a major cause of sudden cardiac death. Optogenetic defibrillation has emerged as a promising anti-arrhythmic strategy based on photoactivation of channelrhodopsins, yet the efficacy and potential adverse consequences of activating different classes of light-gated ion channels have not been systematically evaluated. Previous studies assessed either cation (e.g. ChR2) or anion (e.g. GtACR1) non-selective channels, both of which depolarize resting cardiomyocytes. Recently identified light-gated K+ channels (e.g. WiChR) suppress cardiomyocyte activity while maintaining the membrane potential near its resting state, but their defibrillation potential remains unexplored. We used computational simulations to compare ChR2, GtACR1 and WiChR. Single-cell simulations showed that ChR2 and GtACR1 markedly increased diastolic intracellular Ca2+ (by 42.6% and 52.6%, respectively), whereas WiChR induced only minimal changes (by 4.0%), suggesting lower pro-arrhythmogenic risk. WiChR, however, slightly increased intracellular Na+ (by 15.1% vs. 0.1% and 3.4% for ChR2 and GtACR1), consistent with the residual Na+ permeability of currently available K+-conducting channelrhodopsins. Simulations in human ventricles and atria showed that GtACR1 most effectively terminated re-entrant arrhythmias at low light intensities, whereas WiChR achieved comparable efficacy at ≥5 mW/mm2. Complementary tissue-scale simulations revealed that defibrillation occurs through depolarization within the excitable gap followed by fast Na+ channel inactivation (ChR2 and partly GtACR1), or through reduced membrane resistance at sufficiently high light intensities (large-conductance channels GtACR1 and WiChR). Overall, we demonstrate that channelrhodopsin ion selectivity is a key determinant of both optogenetic defibrillation efficacy and its underlying mechanisms. TRANSLATIONAL PERSPECTIVE: Re-entrant arrhythmias are a leading cause of sudden cardiac death. Standard therapies, including the activation of implantable cardioverter-defibrillators, carry burdens such as pain and myocardial injury. Optogenetic defibrillation has been suggested as a potentially gentler alternative, replacing high-voltage electrical shocks by photoactivation of light-gated ion channels expressed in cardiac cells. Determining which light-gated ion channel optimally balances efficacy and adverse effects on cardiac electrophysiology is critical for clinical translation. We addressed this question using computational simulations and validation experiments in zebrafish hearts. We identify ion selectivity as a key determinant of both the success rate for arrhythmia termination and the underlying mechanisms: non-selective cation- and anion-conducting channels terminate re-entry efficiently while promoting intracellular Ca2+ accumulation, a plausible substrate for pro-arrhythmic side effects that could undermine therapeutic safety. K+-conducting channels largely avoid this pro-arrhythmic risk by holding cardiomyocytes near their resting potential, albeit at the cost of requiring transmural illumination, while also causing mild Na+ accumulation attributable to residual Na+ permeability. Together, these trade-offs define clear clinical implications: K+-conducting channels may provide the safest option for optogenetic defibrillation, particularly once variants with further enhanced K+ selectivity and red-shifted activation spectra for improved operation within blood-perfused myocardium become available. KEY POINTS: We use computational simulations to compare cation non-selective (ChR2), anion non-selective (GtACR1) and K+-selective (WiChR) channelrhodopsins for optogenetic termination of re-entrant arrhythmia. Single-cardiomyocyte simulations suggest that ChR2 and GtACR1 activation can cause progressive accumulation of intracellular Ca2+, which is minimized when using WiChR. Intracellular microelectrode recordings from hearts of transgenic adult zebrafish validate the simulated voltage dynamics seen with ChR2, GtACR1 or WiChR activation. Simulations of human left ventricles and atria indicate that GtACR1 is most effective in terminating re-entrant arrhythmia at low light intensities, whereas WiChR becomes similarly effective at higher intensities. Tissue-scale simulations indicate distinct defibrillation mechanisms: excitable gap extinction by de novo action potential initiation followed by inactivation of fast Na+ channels for depolarizing channelrhodopsins (ChR2, GtACR1), and reduction in membrane resistance for the large-conductance channels (GtACR1, WiChR), effectively clamping the membrane potential at each channel's reversal potential at high light levels.

英文摘要

BACKGROUND: Most atrial septal defects (ASDs) occur sporadically, but familial cases can occur and carry a 10% risk of transmission to offspring. CASE SUMMARY: We describe a 3-generation family with autosomal dominant secundum ASD and conduction system disease related to a novel NKX2-5 variant (c.300del; p.Asp101Thrfs∗75). The index patient, a 58-year-old woman with prior ASD repair and mitral valve replacement, presented with chronotropic incompetence, atrial fibrillation, left bundle branch block, and prolonged pauses, leading to pacemaker implantation. Genetic testing revealed a novel NKX2-5 pathogenic variant in all 3 patients. REVIEW SUMMARY: The combination of an ASD and conduction system disease represents the most common phenotype in patients with pathogenic NKX2-5 variants. TAKE-HOME MESSAGE: A thorough family history and genetic testing are needed when evaluating patients with ASD and concurrent conduction system abnormalities.
